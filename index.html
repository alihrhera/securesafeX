Cloud password managers put everyone's vaults in one place

They're convenient, and they're encrypted. But they also mean your most sensitive data sits on servers you don't control, next to millions of other vaults.

A cloud manager

Your SecureSafeX

One target, millions of vaults

A single server is worth attacking because of how much it holds. If encrypted vaults get copied, attackers can try to crack them offline for as long as they like.

You have to trust what you can't see

Their servers, their staff, their update pipeline, their next acquisition. You never see any of it, and your passwords depend on all of it.

Your access is a subscription

Miss a payment, get locked out of the account, or the service changes its terms, and getting into your own passwords gets harder.

This isn't hypothetical. In 2022, attackers stole encrypted customer vault backups from a major cloud password manager. Users with weak master passwords are still exposed today.

A password vault you can hold in your hand

SecureSafeX is a small USB-C device that stores your passwords and 2FA codes encrypted on the device itself. There's no server, no account, and nothing to sync.

  1. Unlock it on the device

    You enter your PIN with the device's own buttons, so your computer never sees it.

  2. Pick the account

    Scroll to the login you need on the built-in screen.

  3. Press, and it types for you

    The device acts as a USB keyboard and types the username, the password, or both. No drivers, no app.

The questions people ask first

What happens if I lose it?

Whoever finds it gets an encrypted, PIN-locked device, not your passwords. To get your vault back, we're designing an encrypted backup, either to a second device or to an encrypted file you keep. When you sign up, you can tell us which one you'd prefer.

Is it really offline?

In daily use, yes. It talks to your computer only as a USB keyboard. Wi-Fi turns on only when you pick Manage mode to import or edit, and then it runs its own private hotspot between the device and your computer. It never sends your vault to a server. If you want, Wi-Fi can also set the clock that 2FA codes depend on.

How do my existing passwords get onto it?

Export a CSV file from your current manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, Chrome, and others), then import it through the companion browser extension while the device is in Manage mode. The device encrypts everything as it saves it.

Will it work with my phone?

It should work with anything that accepts a USB keyboard: Windows, macOS, Linux, and many Android phones and iPads with USB-C. We're testing compatibility now, and Bluetooth is on the roadmap.

Why should I trust a new device?

The design removes the biggest risk on purpose: there's no server holding your data, so there's nothing for us to leak. Your vault is encrypted on the device and locked with a PIN you enter on the device itself.

How much will it cost, and when will it ship?

We haven't set a price yet. What you tell us in the signup form will shape it. It will be a one-time purchase with no subscription, and people on the waitlist get first access to the first batch.
